# Will Maker: terms of use, draft for counsel

Not for publication as it stands. Written from what the tool does, so counsel
can mark it up rather than start from nothing. Square brackets are decisions
that are not ours to make.

## What the tool is, in one paragraph

Will Maker helps a person put the instructions for their own will into a
standard form and produces a document they print, sign before two witnesses and
keep. It is a drafting aid. It does not give legal advice, does not know the
person's whole situation, and is not a substitute for an advocate. The wording
of the will comes from a fixed set of clauses, reviewed by [advocate, enrolment
no., date: **not yet reviewed**]. Nothing the person types is turned into a
clause by software that has not been reviewed.

## Draft terms

1. **What you get.** A draft will and a signing guide, as a PDF and a Word
   file, made on your device from what you entered. It is not a will until you
   sign it in the presence of two witnesses, who each sign in your presence.
2. **Who it is for.** Adults of sound mind, writing their own will, who are
   not Muslim and do not live in Goa, and whose affairs are not out of the
   ordinary. The tool says when it thinks you are outside that (a check on the
   Checks tab). If it says "take advice", take advice.
3. **Not legal advice.** We are not your advocate, there is no advocate-client
   relationship between you and us by your using the tool, and the tool does
   not check that what you have written is what you meant or is the best way to
   achieve it. [Counsel: the wording that is enforceable for this.]
4. **Your responsibility.** You decide what goes in the will. You are
   responsible for its accuracy: names, addresses, shares, the assets you own.
   Witnesses must not be anyone who benefits under the will. If the tool says
   something needs fixing, fix it or take advice before signing.
5. **Draft until you sign.** Until you finish and fix the open checks the
   tool marks the output as a draft. The marking is a safeguard, not a promise
   that a clean output is valid.
6. **The law changes.** The rules the tool applies are those of the Indian
   Succession Act, 1925 as we understood them on [rules checked on date, from
   `LAW.rulesCheckedOn`]. We re-check them [how often: **to decide**] but you
   should not rely on a draft made long ago without reading it again.
7. **Where your will is kept.** On your device, and in any backup you download.
   If you sign in and lock it with a passcode, an encrypted copy is kept in your
   account that we cannot read. If you forget the passcode we cannot recover the
   will. Keep a backup. See the privacy notice.
8. **Help from an advocate.** If you ask for help we will write back. [Whether,
   when and for what fee a service exists: **not yet**. Until then the form is a
   request, not a contract, and nothing is promised.]
9. **Liability.** [Counsel: cap and exclusions, subject to what cannot be
   excluded. Include loss caused by an invalid or disputed will, loss of the
   device or passcode, and reliance on an old draft.]
10. **Misuse.** Do not use the tool to make a will for someone else without
    their instruction, or to pass off the output as the work of an advocate.
